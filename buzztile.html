BUZZTILE · EXTENDED PROJECT QUALIFICATION (EPQ) · SOLO PROJECT

BuzzTile

Can we walk our way into a greener future? A honeycomb floor tile that turns footsteps into renewable energy, and an app that turns that energy into a game.

ROLE
Everything: research, circuit, code, CAD, fabrication, app
TIMELINE
2025
TYPE
Extended Project Qualification (EPQ)
SKILLS
Circuit design · ESP32 & C++ · Fusion 360 · CNC & laser cutting · HTML, CSS, JS · Gamification

WHAT I MADE

  • A 1:7 scale floor mat of seven hexagon tiles with piezoelectric sensors inside
  • An ESP32 circuit that counts every step and shows it live on an LCD
  • A web app where your steps grow a beehive, unlock bees, and add up for the whole school

02 · THE PROBLEM

Sustainability feels invisible

Everyone learns about climate change in school, but almost no one has ever seen a greenhouse gas or felt their carbon footprint. The causes stay invisible, and the problem feels so big that it’s hard to believe one person can make a difference.

At my school, sustainability was a few pages in a textbook. Being told to recycle and turn off the lights wasn’t going to spark real change. So I asked: what if a footstep, something everyone does all day, could make energy you can actually see?

MY DESIGN BRIEF

  1. Make energy from footstepsConvert steps into measurable electrical energy.
  2. Show it in real timeDetect each step and display the data instantly.
  3. Easy and fun to useAn engaging, intuitive interface for ages 6–18.
  4. Spark curiosityGet people asking questions about sustainability.

03 · RESEARCH

Why piezoelectric?

I researched three areas: energy-harvesting technologies, piezoelectric circuits, and gamification in education. I compared four ways to turn motion into electricity:

Pressing a crystal separates its positive and negative charges, so stress becomes electricity directly.

Why it won: no extra moving parts, small, cheap, easy to find, and well documented. Perfect for a small prototype.

Trade-off: the sensors wear down under repeated impact.

A footstep moves a magnet past a coil, inducing a current. This is how Pavegen’s tiles work.

Why not: proven in public spaces, but it needs many mechanical parts and expensive installation.

Steps compress air, which drives a generator.

Why not: the highest efficiency of the four, but the pumps are bulky and the system is complex for a prototype.

Pressure lets electrons cross an insulating barrier, changing resistance.

Why not: output is only microvolts, so it works as a pressure sensor, not a power source.

The piezo circuits I found all used a bridge rectifier and capacitor to steady the voltage, but they only measured power. None of them gave people anything to interact with. That gap became my project: keep the proven circuit, and add a reason to keep stepping.

04 · IDEATION

Why a honeycomb

I sketched mats shaped like flowers, paw prints, clovers, and a treasure hunt across different locations. Most looked fun but weren’t modular. The honeycomb was the one that could keep growing: hexagons tile perfectly, so a school could add one more cell at a time.

The bee theme became the story. One bee can’t do much, but a hive can. It’s the same with footsteps.

05 · BUILDING IT

Three weeks of a blank screen

The goal was simple: piezo sensors make voltage when stepped on, a microcontroller catches the spikes, and an LCD shows them live. The first time I switched it on, the screen stayed blank and nothing was detected. Here’s what it took to fix it:

  1. Voltage almost undetectableThe sensors were wired in series. I rewired them in parallel.
  2. Readings kept cutting outTwisted wires weren’t enough. I soldered every joint.
  3. Screen still blankI’d assumed two 100 Ω resistors equal one 10 kΩ. They don’t. I swapped in the right one.
  4. Too many wires to trustI replaced the 16-pin LCD with an I2C module: four wires instead of twelve.
  5. Two boards doing one jobI dropped the Arduino and let the ESP32 handle everything, from reading steps to Wi-Fi.

Once it worked, I designed the tiles in Fusion 360 and built a 1:7 scale model to save material and cost. Then I spent two weeks at a local makerspace cutting, soldering and sanding.

07 · THE APP

Every step powers a digital beehive

A number on an LCD isn’t very exciting, so I designed an app to go with it. My research showed that feedback and rewards are what keep people motivated, so I built them into three tabs:

⬡Hive

Steps and volts, live. The hive grows a new chamber as the school walks, with fun facts to unlock along the way.

⬡Bees

13 bees to collect, from common Worker Bees to legendary ones hidden until 5,000 steps.

⬡Stats

Daily, weekly and monthly totals for the whole school, so it feels like a team effort.

This was my first time connecting hardware data to a front end. I learned from YouTube tutorials and short online courses, then built it with HTML, CSS Grid and JavaScript, using the DOM to update the screen whenever a step lands. The browser’s localStorage keeps the data even after a refresh.

08 · TESTING

Tested at school

I set BuzzTile up at school and invited students, teachers and passersby to try it, then interviewed them while they used it.

Ages 6–18students across my whole target range
6 subjectsteachers from Design Technology, Business, English, Maths, Physics and PE
1 focus groupwith A-level Design Technology students, for the technical critique

Most people found it fun and eye-catching, and some said it made them want to learn more about the technology themselves. It started conversations, which is exactly what it was for.

09 · TAKEAWAYS

What I’m taking with me

  1. Most of the work is the part nobody sees. The final tile looks clean, but there were so many things that went wrong, and times I really felt like giving up. It taught me independence.
  2. Know your limits, then design around them. Piezo sensors aren’t made for constant heavy impact. Mine started at 3–5 volts a step and dropped noticeably in just three months.
  3. Next: full size, real hallways. Tougher materials or a hybrid system, then a full-scale test in a school corridor. Done properly, I can see it in schools, parks and airports.
“Design isn’t just about solving problems. It’s about reimagining systems, connecting people, and making invisible forces like energy and climate visible and human.”
